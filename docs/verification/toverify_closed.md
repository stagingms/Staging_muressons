## T-016 and T-085 | LOC=Ch 8 §8.1.2 | Mendelow power/interest grid — primary citation
**VERDICT: CORRECTED. This is the most consequential finding in this pass.**

The primary source exists and was read in full:

Mendelow, A. L. (1981) "Environmental Scanning — The Impact of the Stakeholder Concept."
*Proceedings of the Second International Conference on Information Systems (ICIS 1981)*,
pp. 407-417. Author affiliation given on the paper: Penn State University.
URL fetched (record): https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis1981/20/
URL fetched (full text PDF): https://aisel.aisnet.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1009&context=icis1981

**BUT the paper does not contain a power/interest grid.** The two-by-two in the paper is Figure 2,
titled **"The Power Dynamism Matrix for Environmental Scanning."** Its axes are:
- vertical: **Power** (HIGH / LOW)
- horizontal: **Dynamism** (Dynamic / Static)

The word "interest" does not appear as an axis label. The four quadrants prescribe a scanning
regime — continuous, irregular, periodic or nil — not the "Keep Satisfied / Manage Closely /
Monitor / Keep Informed" treatment strategies the grid is taught with.

Supporting phrases read in the paper: on dynamism, "environmental dynamism contributes
significantly more to decision makers' perceived uncertainty"; on power, "power provides its
possessor with the ability to restructure situations."

**What this means for the book.** The near-universal textbook attribution of the power/INTEREST
grid to Mendelow (1981) is not supported by Mendelow (1981). The paper is about how much scanning
effort to spend on a stakeholder, given the stakeholder's power and the volatility of its
environment. The power/interest version, with its four management prescriptions, is a later
adaptation whose author this pass did not establish.

**Recommendation.** Chapter 8 Draft 1.1 currently uses the tool and declines the citation in text.
That decision is CORRECT and should stand. If a citation is wanted, the honest form is:
"the grid is commonly attributed to Mendelow (1981), whose paper in fact plots power against
environmental dynamism; the power/interest form taught here is a later adaptation."
Do NOT print "Mendelow (1981)" as the source of a power/interest grid.

## T-001 | LOC=Ch 1 | Integrated Reporting six capitals

## T-059 | LOC=Ch 9 | CSDDD penalty cap and due diligence chain
**VERDICT: PART VERIFIED, PART CORRECTED. The chain claim in the register is NOT supported.**

Same instrument, Directive (EU) 2026/470 (URL above).

**(a) Penalty cap — the "3%" reading is right; "the harmonised cap was removed" is WRONG.**
Recital (48): "Member States should be required to set a uniform maximum limit of pecuniary
penalties of 3 % of the net worldwide turnover."
So the cap is not abolished. It is HARMONISED DOWNWARD, from the CSDDD's original floor-of-5%
construction to a uniform maximum of **3% of net worldwide turnover**. The two conflicting
secondary sources the register flagged are reconciled: what was removed is the old requirement to
BASE the maximum on net worldwide turnover at 5%; what replaced it is a uniform 3% ceiling.

**(b) Narrowing the chain to direct business partners — NOT SUPPORTED. Correct this.**
The directive does **not** cut the due diligence obligation back to direct (tier-1) business
partners. Recital (39) keeps the obligation running "across their own operations, those of their
subsidiaries and, where related to their chains of activities, those of their business partners."
What changed is the INFORMATION-GATHERING burden, not the scope of the duty. Recital (41):
companies "should only request information from business partners where that information is
necessary", and for business partners under 5,000 employees only where the information "cannot
reasonably be obtained by other means."

**If Chapter 9 says the chain was narrowed to direct business partners, that sentence is wrong**
and should be rewritten as a limit on information requests down the chain, not a limit on the chain.

**(c) A change the register did not list, and Chapter 9 probably needs it.**
Recital (49): "the specific, Union-wide liability regime currently provided for in Directive (EU)
2024/1760 should be removed." The harmonised EU civil liability regime is gone; Member States
must still ensure "effective access to justice and ... right to an effective remedy" under national
law. Any passage in the book describing a harmonised EU-level civil liability route under the CSDDD
is now out of date.

**Warning:** as with T-058, these are recitals. Article 27 (penalties) and Article 29 (civil
liability) enacting amendments could not be quoted verbatim — the EUR-Lex HTML was truncated on
both attempts. Verify the operative wording on the OJ PDF before printing an article number.

## T-060 | LOC=Ch 5 | SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard V2.0 — Scope 3 and removals
**VERDICT: VERIFIED against the PUBLISHED standard. The draft-derived text can now be replaced.**

*SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard, Version 2.0.* Science Based Targets initiative. **June 2026.**
(cover reads "SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard Version 2.0 | June 2026")
URL fetched (official PDF): https://files.sciencebasedtargets.org/production/files/Corporate-Net-Zero-Standard-version-2.pdf
Release announcement, 11 June 2026: https://sciencebasedtargets.org/news/the-sbti-releases-corporate-net-zero-standard-v2-0-to-accelerate-corporate-climate-action

**Scope 3, as published:**
- Category A companies set Scope 3 targets covering "significant scope 3 categories", the threshold
  being categories at **5% or more** of emissions.
- Three target approaches are available: (i) overarching absolute emissions reduction on a linear
  trajectory to residual levels; (ii) **supplier/customer alignment** — an increasing share of
  tier-1 suppliers or customers "in transition or net-zero aligned"; (iii) category-specific targets
  by commodity (steel, cement and similar) or activity.
- Metrics may be **emissions-based, spend-based or revenue-based**, depending on category and
  approach.
- "limited, justified exclusions" are allowed, e.g. employee commuting and certain leased assets
  outside operational control.

**Carbon removals, as published:**
- Removal targets are **NOT required** for near-term or long-term scope targets. Removals are
  accounted for separately under the GHG Protocol Land Sector and Removals Standard.
- Neutralization of residual emissions is **optional now and intended to become mandatory from
  2035**: "The intention is to mandate ongoing emissions responsibility from 2035, with requirements
  to neutralize residual emissions at net-zero."
- Ongoing-emissions responsibility (removal credits, low-carbon investment, adaptation funding) is
  framed as "a complement, not a substitute" for direct reductions.

**Warning — this is the item most likely to be WRONG in the current manuscript.** The register
records that every Scope 3 and removals description in Chapter 5 currently derives from DRAFT
versions. Two things changed between draft and final that a drafter would easily get backwards:

requirement. Any sentence saying V2.0 "requires companies to set removal targets" or "requires
neutralization of residual emissions" must be corrected. Also note V1.3.1 (April 2026) remains

## T-064 | LOC=Ch 4 §4.2.3 | Severity outranks likelihood for human rights impacts
**VERDICT: VERIFIED — but cite the RIGHT source. The UNGPs themselves do not say this.**

**What the UNGPs actually say.** *Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights: Implementing the
United Nations "Protect, Respect and Remedy" Framework.* United Nations, 2011.
UN document symbol **HR/PUB/11/04**. Endorsed by Human Rights Council resolution 17/4, 16 June 2011.
URL fetched: https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/publications/guidingprinciplesbusinesshr_en.pdf

- **Guiding Principle 24** (p. 26), verbatim: "Where it is necessary to prioritize actions to
  address actual and potential adverse human rights impacts, business enterprises should first seek
  to prevent and mitigate those that are most severe or where delayed response would make them
  irremediable."
- Commentary to GP 24 (p. 26): "Severity is not an absolute concept in this context, but is
  relative to the other human rights impacts the business enterprise has identified."
- **Principle 14** commentary (p. 15): "Severity of impacts will be judged by their scale, scope and
  irremediable character."
- **The UNGP text does not rank severity against LIKELIHOOD.** Likelihood is not addressed in the
  GP 24 commentary. GP 24 contrasts severity with irremediability, not with probability.

**The source that DOES state the rule.** *UN Guiding Principles Reporting Framework*
(developed by Shift and Mazars LLP), on salient human rights issues. It prioritises potential
negative impacts:
> "primarily based on their potential severity … secondarily based on their likelihood, retaining
> due attention to high-severity, low-likelihood impacts"
URL fetched: https://www.ungpreporting.org/resources/salient-human-rights-issues/

**Recommendation.** If §4.2.3 says "severity outranks likelihood", attribute it to the **UN Guiding
Principles Reporting Framework** (Shift and Mazars), not to the UNGPs. If it wants a UN-level
citation, the honest one is GP 24 for the prioritisation rule and Principle 14 commentary for the
scale/scope/irremediability test of severity — and it must not add "likelihood", which is not there.

**Warning.** The clause "retaining due attention to high-severity, low-likelihood impacts" is the
part practitioners drop. A chapter that says only "severity outranks likelihood" loses the operative
half of the rule.
